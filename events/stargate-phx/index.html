You belong on the team

Curious is enough.

Stargate PHX is built for mixed-experience tables. Arrive alone, come with a friend, know every Stargate episode, or know only that the big ring goes somewhere interesting.

Your first tabletop game

You do not need to know what to roll. Say what you want your hero to try; Kyle will connect the idea to the sheet and teach the rule when it becomes useful.

A Stargate fan

You will recognize the rhythm: briefing, gate travel, a strange world, a team problem, and a decision that changes the ending. Canon trivia is never an admission test.

A D&D fifth-edition player

Your d20 instincts still help. Stargate changes how missions, noncombat pressure, teamwork, advancement, firearms, and scene stakes work.

A solo arrival or plus-one

The mission gives everyone a reason to work together. No established group, performance skill, accent, or character voice is needed.

The episode begins

A briefing. A new world. Your team's call.

Phoenix Site sends teams through the Stargate to investigate unfamiliar worlds, meet people with their own priorities, solve technical and human problems, and bring everyone home.

Some missions lean toward exploration or diplomacy. Others add rescue, survival, investigation, or tactical pressure. The goal is not to recite a script—it is to discover what this episode becomes with this team.

No puzzle answers, hidden villains, or required lore are revealed before play.

The series model

Attend one. Skip one. Return later.

Each session is a complete, independently playable mission. Connected details can reward returning players, but missing an episode does not remove you from the story.

  1. 01Drop in

    Join a later mission with a concise briefing. No campaign catch-up is required.

  2. 02Complete the episode

    One session reaches a satisfying ending even if this is your only visit.

  3. 03Return when it works

    Skip an episode without losing your place. Past missions may return as reruns.

Around the table

You are inside the episode—not watching it.

Study the briefing, choose an approach, step through the gate, and respond to a world that does not wait politely.

You might examine a strange device, read a tense negotiation, scout a route, treat an injured ally, find cover, or turn a bad plan into the answer nobody expected.

The fun comes from sharing information, building on one another's ideas, and making a call before every variable is known. Combat can happen, but it is one kind of pressure—not the only way to matter.

What to expect

Clear starts. Real choices. No performance test.

Rules arrive when useful

Kyle teaches the active rule at the moment it matters. Questions are expected; you do not need to hide confusion or study before arriving.

Describe—do not perform

Speak in character, describe an idea in your own words, point to the sheet, or ask for help. Acting and character voices are optional.

A team, not seven solo stories

Kyle manages pace and spotlight, invites quieter approaches, and keeps one strong personality from becoming the whole mission.

Comfort is part of play

Expect a brief safety check-in, permission to pause or step away, and a clear path to flag content without having to justify the boundary.

Optional depth

FAQ

The fast answers.

Do I need tabletop roleplaying experience?

No. First-time players are expected. Kyle teaches the active rules as they become useful.

Do I need to know Stargate?

No. The mission briefing supplies what your hero knows. Fans will recognize the setting, but canon trivia is never required to solve the episode.

Do I need to prepare or read the rules?

No. Premade heroes are ready, and all rules reading is optional. Use it only if previewing helps you feel comfortable.

Do I have to act or use a character voice?

No. Describe what your hero tries in your own words. Voices, accents, and first-person dialogue are optional.

Can I come for just one session?

Yes. Every episode is independently playable and designed to reach a complete, satisfying ending.

What if I miss an episode?

Join a later mission. Connected details can reward returners, but missing an episode does not lock you out, and some missions may return as reruns.

Are characters provided?

Yes. Seven premade heroes cover diplomacy, engineering, medicine, science, scouting, protection, leadership, and piloting.

Can I bring a custom character?

Possibly, with advance approval. Ask Kyle before the session so the character can be checked for the mission, level, rules version, and team.

Is this just D&D fifth edition with a Stargate skin?

It shares a familiar d20 foundation, but mission advancement, Determination, Tension, noncombat procedures, technology, firearms, and team roles change how play works.

Is combat required?

Missions can include tactical danger, but investigation, diplomacy, rescue, technical problem-solving, exploration, and teamwork also drive outcomes. Exact content notes will accompany each scheduled session.

How long is a session?

Plan for roughly 4–5 hours. Current missions begin at 10:30 AM; confirm the details for your selected date on the official Mox listing.

Where do I register?

Registration is open through the official Mox event listing. Choose August 22, September 5, or September 19 when you book.

What if I have an access or content concern?

Send Kyle a note before the session when possible. He will make a best-effort plan and confirm what the table and venue can support.
