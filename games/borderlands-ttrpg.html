Borderlands TTRPG

Loot, builds, and second chances rebuilt for a d20 table.

A pre-AI fan tabletop adaptation that translated Borderlands-style gear, class choices, shops, enemies, vehicles, and downed-state heroics into runnable table procedures.

Pre-AI system design Custom d20 adaptation 183-page source packet

What This Adaptation Shows

Borderlands TTRPG shows the same design habit from a different angle: take a videogame loop people understand, then build enough table structure that the GM can actually run it.

The case is fan adaptation and system-design proof, not an official or commercial Borderlands product. The value is in the mechanics translation: loot tables, gear slots, downed-state action, encounter rewards, shop prep, and player inventory that all connect.

  • Turns constant loot discovery into procedural tables and player-facing trackers.
  • Connects NPCs, shops, vehicles, encounters, rewards, feats, and progression.
  • Shows dense pre-AI system work built from scratch for a d20 table.

What to notice

The project does not stop at rules text. It gives players a place to track loot, gear pressure, effects, health, and inventory constraints.

Game craft

A loot shooter loop translated into table operations.

The strongest part of this case is operational: players needed gear choices, the GM needed reward procedures, and the table needed the loop to keep moving.

What I built

Custom d20 rules, Fight For Your Life and Second Chance procedures, Badass Points, shields, damage types, weapons, mods, feats, classes, and vehicle rules.

What the table tracked

Player inventories, backpack capacity, mod slots, HP/VHP, shops, enemy drops, encounter loot, rewards, vehicles, and leveling stayed connected through sheets.

03

What it proves

Kyle can translate a high-volume videogame economy into usable tabletop procedures without relying on hand-waved GM judgment for every reward.
