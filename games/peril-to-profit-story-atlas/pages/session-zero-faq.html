Session Zero FAQ

Short answers players can read while the table is getting ready. This page avoids GM scripts and hidden structure.

Open public FAQ URL Back to Session Zero Safety Tools

What is a tabletop roleplaying game?

You and the GM tell a story together. You play one character. The GM plays the world. You describe what you want to do, dice help decide uncertain moments, and the story changes because of the table's choices.

What is a GM, and what do I do as a player?

The GM frames scenes, portrays the world, makes rulings, and keeps spotlight moving. You describe your character's actions, ask questions, make choices, and help the other players have a good time too.

What is this one-shot?

Peril to Profit: The Goldspire Messengers is a fantasy delivery one-shot. You are hired to escort a sealed package through Sablewood to Hush, and the job becomes more complicated than the contract suggests.

Who is my GM?
Your GM is Kyle, he/him. Kyle has 15+ years running in-person and online TTRPGs. His Daggerheart style is story-first and roleplay-friendly: mechanics support the table's fun and help resolve risky moments. You can learn more here: https://jonathankhobson.github.io/g/.
Do I need to know Daggerheart?

No. The table will teach the rules as they matter. Most of the time, describe what your character does, roll when the outcome is risky or dramatic, and ask questions whenever something is unclear.

How is Daggerheart different from D&D?

For this table, the big differences are the Duality Dice, Hope and Fear, narrative spotlight instead of a fixed initiative ladder, and character tags like Experiences mattering directly in play. You do not need D&D knowledge to play.

What am I supposed to do on my turn?

Describe what your character wants, tries, asks, notices, risks, or helps with. If a roll is needed, the GM will name the trait. If you do not know what to do, say you are stuck and the table will help.

Can I really say or do anything?

You can propose anything that fits the fiction and respects the table. The GM may say yes, ask for a roll, offer a cost, clarify the stakes, or adjust the action so it fits the scene and safety boundaries.

How do I know if I succeed or fail?

When the outcome is uncertain, you roll, add the relevant trait, and compare the total to the difficulty. Hope/Fear color the result, and matching Duality Dice create a Critical Success.

How does the Duality Dice system work?
Daggerheart is narrative fantasy. The GM describes the world, players describe what their characters do, and we roll when the outcome is risky, uncertain, or dramatic.
  • Describe what your character tries to do.
  • If it is risky or uncertain, roll Hope and Fear d12s.
  • Add the trait that fits your approach.
  • Tell the GM the total and whether Hope or Fear was higher.
  • We will learn the rest as it matters.
What are Hope and Fear?

Hope is player momentum and fuel for help, Experiences, and some abilities. Fear is GM pressure used to complicate scenes, trigger danger, or make the world react. Fear is not punishment; it keeps the story alive.

What are the six traits?

Agility, Strength, Finesse, Instinct, Presence, and Knowledge. The GM tells you which trait fits your approach. Say what your character does first, then find the trait that best matches it.

What are domain cards and abilities?

They are the special options on your sheet and cards. You do not need to memorize them. When you want to do something special, point to the card or ask what it means and we will resolve it together.

What are Experiences?

Experiences are specific background tags from your character's life. When one clearly helps, you can spend Hope before the roll to add its value.

What is Stress vs HP?

Hit Points track serious harm. Stress tracks strain, pressure, or being pushed too hard. Both matter, and the GM will tell you when to mark them.

What does Armor do?

Armor Slots can reduce incoming damage severity when you mark them. They are limited, so ask before marking one if you are unsure.

How do I read my character sheet?
In front of you: a character sheet, quick rules card, connection sheet, and safety card. Your sheet tells you who your character is, what they are good at, what they can do, and how much pressure or harm they can take.
  • Look for: name and vibe, traits, Experiences, weapons/actions, Hope, Stress, Hit Points, Armor, and class/domain abilities.
  • Pick or confirm a character. If you chose ahead of time, make sure the sheet in front of you matches that choice.

Open the Character Sheet Guide

Can I customize my character?

Yes, lightly. In a short one-shot, rename, choose pronouns, adjust look and vibe, and clarify a motive. Major stat, class, card, damage, or balance changes need dedicated build time.

How do connections work?
Offer, don't overwrite. A connection prompt is your character's perspective: what they remember, saw, believed, misunderstood, felt, or interpreted.
  • The other player can accept it, clarify it, complicate it, soften it, or pass.
  • Yes, and...
  • Yes, but...
  • Can we soften that?
How does tonight flow?
We will start with Session Zero, move into the delivery job, follow the route through escalating complications, and take breaks so the table can breathe.
  • Session Zero: safety, rules, characters, and connections.
  • Opening contract: the sealed delivery and why you took the job.
  • Road trouble: the route proves less safe than advertised.
  • Hush and investigation: meet people, ask questions, and decide who to trust.
  • Final pressure: the delivery becomes a choice, not just a job.
  • Breaks: short five-minute pauses as needed, plus a longer midpoint break if the table wants it.
What is the rating?
Think adult PG-13-ish fantasy adventure. There will be combat and danger, but violence descriptions stay light unless the table chooses to make a moment more intense.
  • This is an adult game, but not a grimdark or graphic game.
  • Likely content: combat, peril, ambushes, monsters, undead, corporate exploitation, fear or memory magic, and morally complicated choices.
  • Animal peril may appear. Violence against animals is optional and can be softened, avoided, or redirected.
  • If anything I describe or any action at the table makes you uncomfortable, say pause, slow down, rewind, or break.
  • The goal is to explore fantasy danger together while keeping the real table safe and communicative.
How do safety and breaks work?
You do not need to justify a boundary. If something is not fun, too intense, or not for tonight, we can pause, redirect, fade to black, or move on.
  • Open Door: you can step away at any point and return when ready.
  • Simple table signal: say pause, slow down, can we rewind, or break.
  • If you missed something, ask me to repeat it.
  • If I am moving too fast, ask me to slow down.
  • If you are confused, ask any question at any time. There are no dumb questions here.
  • If a topic is okay in the story but not for your character, say not for me.
  • Discord or text can help between scenes, but I may not see it immediately while running the table.
What does the table expect from me?
This works best when everyone is engaged, respectful, and helping the table have fun. You are responsible for your own fun and for noticing the other people at the table too.
  • Engage with the story and ask questions.
  • Invite other players in: ask them what their character thinks, notices, or does.
  • Speak when it is your turn, and be mindful of how much space you are taking.
  • Only participate at the level you are comfortable with.
  • Tell me if you are confused, uncomfortable, not having fun, or need help.
  • I will do my best to engage everyone, but the table works because we all pay attention to each other.
  • If there is time at the end, we may do quick Stars and Wishes: one thing you liked, one thing you would want more or different next time.
What if I am confused, freeze, or worry I am slowing the group down?

Ask immediately, or say you are stuck. You can ask the GM to repeat something, slow down, explain a rule, point to the sheet, offer options, or come back to you after another player acts.

What if something feels uncomfortable?

Say pause, slow down, can we rewind, or break. You can also step away at any time. You do not need to justify the boundary at the table.

#rules-canon

Action Roll Flow

['roll', 'check', 'skill check', 'action rolls'] ['saving throw', 'save', 'reaction']

Ask what the PC does, choose the trait that matches the fictional approach, set or reveal the Difficulty, then roll.

Roll Duality Dice, add the trait and any applicable Experience or bonus, compare the total to the Difficulty, then read Hope/Fear spin.

Roll only when the outcome is uncertain and the consequence matters. If there is no meaningful pressure, give the result without a roll.

#rules-canon

Duality Dice

['2d12', 'hope die', 'fear die']

Official default: roll two d12s, one Hope die and one Fear die.

Total the dice, add the relevant trait and bonuses, then compare to Difficulty.

The higher die determines whether the roll is with Hope or with Fear. Matching dice are a Critical Success.

Reaction rolls use the Duality Dice structure, but they do not generate Hope or Fear.

#rules-canon

Hope/Fear Outcomes

TotalSpinResult
>= DifficultyHopeSuccess with Hope: get what you want and gain Hope.
>= DifficultyFearSuccess with Fear: get what you want, but there is a cost or consequence; GM gains Fear.
< DifficultyHopeFailure with Hope: things do not go as planned, but gain Hope.
< DifficultyFearFailure with Fear: things go badly; major consequence or complication; GM gains Fear.

#rules-canon

Critical Success

['crit', 'critical', 'doubles']

When the Hope die and Fear die match, the roll is a Critical Success.

A Critical Success counts as a roll with Hope even if the total would otherwise miss the Difficulty.

On a Critical Success, the PC gains Hope and clears 1 Stress. On an attack roll, follow the official critical damage rules.

D&D-to-Daggerheart Translation

D&D Conversion

Daggerheart uses traits, not D&D skills. Pick the trait that matches the player's fictional approach.

Use the D&D conversion page as a GM aid for players arriving from 5e; it is not official Daggerheart rules text.

#rules-canon

Advantage / Disadvantage

['advantage', 'disadvantage']

Advantage adds a d6 advantage die to the roll total.

Disadvantage subtracts a d6 disadvantage die from the roll total.

Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out before rolling. If multiple advantage dice remain, use the highest one.

The GM may grant advantage or disadvantage when the fiction supports it.

#rules-canon

Experiences, Help, Tag Team

Experiences are short backstory/expertise phrases. Spend 1 Hope before a roll to add a relevant Experience modifier.

Help an Ally: spend 1 Hope, describe feasible help, roll a d6 advantage die, and add it to the acting PC's roll using the normal advantage rules.

Tag Team Roll: once per session, spend 3 Hope to combine two PCs' efforts. Both roll, choose one result for both, and resolve as a single action roll.

#rules-canon

Hope Uses / Fear Uses

['with Hope', 'hope spend', 'spend Hope'] ['Sablewood Hope Moments', 'Sablewood Fear Moments', 'hope moments', 'fear moments']

Hope can be spent to Help an Ally, use Experiences, initiate a Tag Team Roll, or activate Hope Features.

On a roll with Fear, the GM gains Fear and makes a move reflecting the complication.

Fear can be spent to spotlight adversaries, use Fear Features, make stronger GM moves, apply adversary Experiences, and advance pressure when the rules or stat block call for it.

#rules-canon

GM Moves / Adversary Spotlight

['GM Move', 'make a move'] ['rules drawer', 'quick reference'] GM MovesAdversary Spotlight

The spotlight moves through the fiction rather than a fixed initiative order.

The GM commonly makes moves when a PC rolls with Fear or fails an action roll.

Use softer moves to telegraph, reveal, or reposition. Use harder moves when the fiction and result call for direct consequence.

Spend Fear or use features when the adversary/environment rules call for it.

#rules-canon

Adversary Attack Rolls

['enemy attack', 'attack roll', 'adversary attack'] Adversary Attack Rolls

Adversaries roll a d20 plus their attack modifier against the target PC's Evasion.

PCs do not roll defense by default. Use reaction rolls only when a rule, feature, or GM call gives one.

PC action rolls against adversaries use the adversary's Difficulty from the stat block.

#rules-canon

Damage / Armor / Stress

['damage', 'threshold', 'thresholds'] ['damage die', 'damage dice', 'weapon damage'] Damage / Armor

Compare incoming damage to the character's damage thresholds to determine how many HP are marked.

Mark an Armor Slot to reduce the severity of incoming damage by one threshold.

When a character marks their last Stress, they become Vulnerable until they clear at least 1 Stress.

If a character must mark Stress while all Stress slots are marked, follow the official overflow consequence from the character rules.

#rules-canon

Short Rest

['rest', 'Prepare', 'Tend to Wounds', 'Clear Stress', 'Repair Armor']

A short rest is about an hour to catch breath and recover.

Each PC may swap domain cards between loadout and vault, then choose two moves, including choosing the same move twice.

Short rest moves: Tend to Wounds, Clear Stress, Repair Armor, or Prepare.

Prepare gains Hope; preparing with one or more party members gives each of those PCs 2 Hope.

#rules-canon + module tracker

Countdown

['clock', 'timer', 'countdown']

Countdowns are visible pressure clocks. Tell players what changes when a countdown ticks.

On progress countdowns, successful action rolls can tick the party closer to the goal.

On consequence countdowns, failures or success with Fear can tick the opposing pressure closer.

Open Vale uses a module ritual countdown as a tracker; do not treat it as a new condition.

#rules-canon

Ranges

['range', 'movement', 'distance']

Use Daggerheart range bands at the table: Melee, Very Close, Close, Far, Very Far, and Out of Range.

When exact distance is not important, keep ranges conversational and tied to reachable action.

#rules-canon

Official Conditions

Official Conditions

Daggerheart has three primary global conditions: Hidden, Restrained, and Vulnerable.

Some features create unique effects, but those are feature-specific text, not module-wide conditions.

Unless a rule says otherwise, the same condition cannot stack on one target.

Temporary conditions can be cleared with a fitting action roll, a GM-approved fictional method, or by an adversary spending its spotlight to clear the condition.

Unconscious appears only as Death Move/status context in this Atlas, not as a global condition card.
