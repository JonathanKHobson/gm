Read-Aloud

The trees break around an old clearing of standing stones, wet grass, and pale ward-light. Official markers call it Open Vale, but the place feels older than the paperwork and more awake than a ruin.

Sensory Palette

  • Sight: Standing stones ring wet grass and ward grooves; old runes and newer markers disagree about what the clearing is for.
  • Sound: The wind falls away near the center, ward-light ticks in stone seams, and every voice comes back slightly lower.
  • Smell: Cold mineral air, wet grass, stone dust, whitefire ozone, and the faint sweetness of crushed clover under lightning.
  • Texture / air: The ground feels firm until the ward hum rises; then each footstep seems to answer through the soles of the boots.
  • Background life: Animals avoid the center but circle the edge. The clearing is not dead; it is bracing for work.
GM Location / Battlefield Notes
GM Orientation

The climax. A perfect circular clearing — the only break in the canopy — where the Custodian first forged Hush's ward pillars. The party takes a Short Rest, then defends her through the ward-renewal ritual against waves of skeletons and Soul-Audit Wraiths while a countdown die ticks. Win condition: survive until the ritual completes, not "kill everything."

    Talk with the Custodian before the ritual

    Trait: — Difficulty: —

    Canon: she describes the site, her near-death forging the pillars, her fear of the Forgotten Gods. RP beat.

    Short Rest moves

    Trait: — Difficulty: —

    Canon (p.34): each PC picks 2 — Tend to Wounds (clear 1d4+1 HP), Clear Stress (1d4+1), Repair Armor (1d4+1 slots), Prepare (gain Hope; 2 if with a party member).

    Read the clearing for danger before it starts

    Trait: Instinct Difficulty: 14

    Success: they sense from which directions the creatures will come — lets a PC pre-position to defend the Custodian.

    Defend the Custodian in the fight

    Trait: Attack/Agility Difficulty: —

    Her Difficulty is 11; she takes only narrative damage; every hit on her ticks the countdown up.

    Fear Pressure
    • Environment Feature — Market Correction (canon): spend a Fear to summon two additional Legacy Skeletons from the ground within Very Close range of a PC. Use it to keep the countdown moving (skeletons die easily → countdown ticks down on each defeat).
    • The Wraiths' Memory Delve and Pass-Through are your big Fear spends — use Pass-Through sparingly (it can lock a PC out; if the whole party goes Untethered they all mark 2 HP and snap back).
    • Spend Fear: the open sky flickers — for one beat the stars rearrange wrongly (Forgotten Gods watching). Narrative dread, no mechanics.
    Combat
    • The ritual countdown (p.35): Set a d8 to 8. Ticks down 1 each time an adversary is defeated; ticks up 1 each time the Custodian is hit. At 0, the ritual completes → Epilogue.
    • Setup (p.35): Custodian standee center; PCs around her; 4 Legacy Skeletons in Close range of her; 2 Soul-Audit Wraiths in Far range.
    • Legacy Skeleton — Tier 1 Standard (×4)
    • Rusted Sword: Melee, 1d6+1 phy · ATK +0 · Difficulty 12 · HP 2 · Stress 1 · Thresholds 7/none
    • Battle guide: roll d20 (no ATK mod) vs Evasion; hit → 1d6+1. Take 6 or less damage → mark 1 HP; otherwise defeated.
    • Group Attack (Feature): spend a Fear, move several skeletons into Melee, one shared attack roll at +0; on success deal 4 phy per skeleton, combined into one hit.
    • Soul-Audit Wraith — Tier 1 Bruiser (×2)
    • Lifedrain: Far, 2d6+8 mag · ATK +3 · Difficulty 13 · HP 6 · Stress 3 · Major 7 / Severe 14
    • Spectral Body (Passive): resistance to physical damage (incoming physical halved, round up).
    • Memory Delve (Action): attack a Close target; on hit, the wraith places a hand on their cheek — ask the player to describe a terrifying childhood moment — then 3d4+9 magic and Vulnerable until next rest.
    • Pass-Through (Action): spend a Fear, attack a Melee target; on success the PC is Untethered (can't act until the countdown ticks down). Whole party Untethered at once → all mark 2 HP, souls return, condition cleared.
    • Open Vale Site — Environment, Tier 1 Exploration
    • Feature Market Correction (Action): spend a Fear to summon 2 Legacy Skeletons within Very Close of a PC.
    • GM guidance (p.35): if the countdown stalls, use Market Correction to spawn easy skeletons. If no PC is in range, adversaries target the Custodian (Difficulty 11, narrative damage only). Death move (Quickstart): a PC at last HP falls unconscious until healed or the danger passes — not dead.
    Transitions
    • In: From the Treehouse (05), Act Five.
    • Out: Countdown hits 0 → ritual completes → the Keystone Asset epilogue (see 07 for the sequel hook).
    Hidden Truth Layer
    • GM-only hidden truth: Soul-Audit Wraiths and Legacy Skeletons read as corporate instruments, not natural spirits.
    • Run Memory Delve as extraction and the skeletons as repossessed/decommissioned labor logic, while keeping the reveal subtextual.

    Long Place Description
    A beautiful clearing with real ground to use, stone for cover, open lanes for sightlines, ward-lines underfoot, and a center worth standing in front of. Pressure can come from every direction at once. The place breaks into usable table spaces: Ritual center: The protected middle holds the Custodian's work. Standing stone ring: Old stones create cover, lanes, and sacred weight. Flowered approach lanes: Open grass paths show where threats can arrive. Ward-line spokes: Glowing lines connect center to surrounding stones. Plants and materials: Sunfire Lily, flowered ward grasses, low healing herbs, moss at stone bases, wind-bent seed heads. Animals and background life: Ward-Moths trace the ward lines, Mist-Things gather at the edge of low fog, field crickets go silent near ward pulses. The air carries ward light, pressure changes, petal movement against the wind, countdown rhythm. The clearing breathes with the ritual: light gathers, pressure rises, life pulls back, then returns if the ward stabilizes.
    Known For
    A perfectly circular clearing older than the painted Open Vale marker staked at its edge. Standing stones ring a center where moss and ward-light still gather. Flowered grass paths run in from every direction. Lines of light spoke out from the middle to the stones, and the company sign looks very small and very recent against all of it.
    Culture / Food
    No market, no stalls, just the clearing and whatever people bring. Offerings of bread and flowers rest at the bases of the stones. Whoever keeps the ritual shares a little honeyed water, blessed mostly by intention.
    Travel Texture
    You smell crushed grass and cold stone before you understand where you are standing. The air pulls taut as the ward-light gathers. Petals drift against the wind, the field crickets cut out, and a slow countdown rhythm settles into your chest whether you want it there or not.

    #rules-canon

    Action Roll Flow

    ['roll', 'check', 'skill check', 'action rolls'] ['saving throw', 'save', 'reaction']

    Ask what the PC does, choose the trait that matches the fictional approach, set or reveal the Difficulty, then roll.

    Roll Duality Dice, add the trait and any applicable Experience or bonus, compare the total to the Difficulty, then read Hope/Fear spin.

    Roll only when the outcome is uncertain and the consequence matters. If there is no meaningful pressure, give the result without a roll.

    #rules-canon

    Duality Dice

    ['2d12', 'hope die', 'fear die']

    Official default: roll two d12s, one Hope die and one Fear die.

    Total the dice, add the relevant trait and bonuses, then compare to Difficulty.

    The higher die determines whether the roll is with Hope or with Fear. Matching dice are a Critical Success.

    Reaction rolls use the Duality Dice structure, but they do not generate Hope or Fear.

    #rules-canon

    Hope/Fear Outcomes

    TotalSpinResult
    >= DifficultyHopeSuccess with Hope: get what you want and gain Hope.
    >= DifficultyFearSuccess with Fear: get what you want, but there is a cost or consequence; GM gains Fear.
    < DifficultyHopeFailure with Hope: things do not go as planned, but gain Hope.
    < DifficultyFearFailure with Fear: things go badly; major consequence or complication; GM gains Fear.

    #rules-canon

    Critical Success

    ['crit', 'critical', 'doubles']

    When the Hope die and Fear die match, the roll is a Critical Success.

    A Critical Success counts as a roll with Hope even if the total would otherwise miss the Difficulty.

    On a Critical Success, the PC gains Hope and clears 1 Stress. On an attack roll, follow the official critical damage rules.

    D&D-to-Daggerheart Translation

    D&D Conversion

    Daggerheart uses traits, not D&D skills. Pick the trait that matches the player's fictional approach.

    Use the D&D conversion page as a GM aid for players arriving from 5e; it is not official Daggerheart rules text.

    #rules-canon

    Advantage / Disadvantage

    ['advantage', 'disadvantage']

    Advantage adds a d6 advantage die to the roll total.

    Disadvantage subtracts a d6 disadvantage die from the roll total.

    Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out before rolling. If multiple advantage dice remain, use the highest one.

    The GM may grant advantage or disadvantage when the fiction supports it.

    #rules-canon

    Experiences, Help, Tag Team

    Experiences are short backstory/expertise phrases. Spend 1 Hope before a roll to add a relevant Experience modifier.

    Help an Ally: spend 1 Hope, describe feasible help, roll a d6 advantage die, and add it to the acting PC's roll using the normal advantage rules.

    Tag Team Roll: once per session, spend 3 Hope to combine two PCs' efforts. Both roll, choose one result for both, and resolve as a single action roll.

    #rules-canon

    Hope Uses / Fear Uses

    ['with Hope', 'hope spend', 'spend Hope'] ['Sablewood Hope Moments', 'Sablewood Fear Moments', 'hope moments', 'fear moments']

    Hope can be spent to Help an Ally, use Experiences, initiate a Tag Team Roll, or activate Hope Features.

    On a roll with Fear, the GM gains Fear and makes a move reflecting the complication.

    Fear can be spent to spotlight adversaries, use Fear Features, make stronger GM moves, apply adversary Experiences, and advance pressure when the rules or stat block call for it.

    #rules-canon

    GM Moves / Adversary Spotlight

    ['GM Move', 'make a move'] ['rules drawer', 'quick reference'] GM MovesAdversary Spotlight

    The spotlight moves through the fiction rather than a fixed initiative order.

    The GM commonly makes moves when a PC rolls with Fear or fails an action roll.

    Use softer moves to telegraph, reveal, or reposition. Use harder moves when the fiction and result call for direct consequence.

    Spend Fear or use features when the adversary/environment rules call for it.

    #rules-canon

    Adversary Attack Rolls

    ['enemy attack', 'attack roll', 'adversary attack'] Adversary Attack Rolls

    Adversaries roll a d20 plus their attack modifier against the target PC's Evasion.

    PCs do not roll defense by default. Use reaction rolls only when a rule, feature, or GM call gives one.

    PC action rolls against adversaries use the adversary's Difficulty from the stat block.

    #rules-canon

    Damage / Armor / Stress

    ['damage', 'threshold', 'thresholds'] ['damage die', 'damage dice', 'weapon damage'] Damage / Armor

    Compare incoming damage to the character's damage thresholds to determine how many HP are marked.

    Mark an Armor Slot to reduce the severity of incoming damage by one threshold.

    When a character marks their last Stress, they become Vulnerable until they clear at least 1 Stress.

    If a character must mark Stress while all Stress slots are marked, follow the official overflow consequence from the character rules.

    #rules-canon

    Short Rest

    ['rest', 'Prepare', 'Tend to Wounds', 'Clear Stress', 'Repair Armor']

    A short rest is about an hour to catch breath and recover.

    Each PC may swap domain cards between loadout and vault, then choose two moves, including choosing the same move twice.

    Short rest moves: Tend to Wounds, Clear Stress, Repair Armor, or Prepare.

    Prepare gains Hope; preparing with one or more party members gives each of those PCs 2 Hope.

    #rules-canon + module tracker

    Countdown

    ['clock', 'timer', 'countdown']

    Countdowns are visible pressure clocks. Tell players what changes when a countdown ticks.

    On progress countdowns, successful action rolls can tick the party closer to the goal.

    On consequence countdowns, failures or success with Fear can tick the opposing pressure closer.

    Open Vale uses a module ritual countdown as a tracker; do not treat it as a new condition.

    #rules-canon

    Ranges

    ['range', 'movement', 'distance']

    Use Daggerheart range bands at the table: Melee, Very Close, Close, Far, Very Far, and Out of Range.

    When exact distance is not important, keep ranges conversational and tied to reachable action.

    #rules-canon

    Official Conditions

    Official Conditions

    Daggerheart has three primary global conditions: Hidden, Restrained, and Vulnerable.

    Some features create unique effects, but those are feature-specific text, not module-wide conditions.

    Unless a rule says otherwise, the same condition cannot stack on one target.

    Temporary conditions can be cleared with a fitting action roll, a GM-approved fictional method, or by an adversary spending its spotlight to clear the condition.

    Unconscious appears only as Death Move/status context in this Atlas, not as a global condition card.
