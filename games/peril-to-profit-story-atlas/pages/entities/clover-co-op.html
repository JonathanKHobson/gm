Player-Facing Description

The Clover Co-op: Warm lamps, clover doors, shared bowls, and dry jokes make the Clover Co-op feel like Hush's stubborn heartbeat. Slogan: Many hands, one Hush.

Imagine This Like

The town diner crossed with a Grange hall, or a village pub where the regulars have assigned stools. Think community potluck, mutual-aid kitchen, and the bar where everyone knows your name.

GM-Only Truth

GM-only: the Co-op holds pieces of resistance and old ward history, but no single NPC must unlock it.

Role in Story

Make hospitality practical, specific, and emotionally risky.

A massive tavern built around an ancient tree, full of evening talk, shoes on a clothing line, small trinkets, and clues disguised as hospitality.

Read-Aloud

Inside the village, the co-op is a warm public room of tea steam, shoe lines, gift shelves, chipped mugs, and people deciding whether these travelers are guests, trouble, or both.

Sensory Palette

  • Sight: Mismatched chairs face inward, shoe lines mark the threshold, and gift shelves hold jars, cloth, ribbons, and careful generosity.
  • Sound: Tea pours, mugs knock softly, someone clears their throat before hard news, and a ward chime outside lands a little flat.
  • Smell: Clover honey, tea steam, wet boot leather, old wood warmed by bodies, and food stretched with pride instead of abundance.
  • Texture / air: Counters are nicked smooth; mugs are chipped in familiar places; wool sleeves stay damp near the door.
  • Background life: The room tracks manners. People make space, stop talking, resume talking, and decide whether the party is safe to feed.

    Player-Safe Long
    The Clover Co-op is not impressive in a capital-city way. It is impressive in the way a table can hold ten people, then somehow hold twelve. Its doors are painted with uneven clovers, its benches remember every argument, and help arrives wrapped in a joke before anyone admits it is help.
    Table Read-Aloud
    The clover on the door is hand-painted and a little lopsided. Inside, lamps burn low and golden, a pot keeps itself warm, and three conversations pause just long enough to measure whether you are guests, trouble, or both.
    Imagine This Like
    Think a rural mutual-aid co-op or a tight-knit fishing village, the town that quietly refuses to sell, a small-town main street with ward-craft. Stubborn neighborly warmth as resistance.
    Sensory Palette
    • warm stew, damp wool, old wood, clover paint, lamp oil, borrowed mugs, and laughter that hides worry without erasing it
    • the comfortable pressure of being watched by people who will feed you and judge you in the same breath
    • a village room where every object seems to belong to someone and everyone
    Operating Places
    • Hush communal rooms, tool sheds, co-op doors, and kitchen-table meetings
    • Clover Co-op markets and favor networks inside Hush
    • quiet aid paths toward Bramble-adjacent families when the table earns that trust
    NPC Roster
    • Fidget: nervous warmth, practical errands, and visible care
    • Lausa Standworth: old memory, ward-craft texture, and earned lore
    • Halython Fives: social play, games, and the moment Hush chooses whether to trust the party
    Interconnections
    • The Bramble Union has sympathy here because Hush remembers people before labels.
    • Zephra Exchange dislikes any village that can meet needs without paying a gatekeeper.
    • Hexmart's cheerful safety products look sillier beside neighbors who actually show up.
    Reveal Cues
    • Surface: show hospitality, humor, food, and local pride.
    • Hint: let small comments reveal Bramble sympathy and anti-contract habits.
    • Earned: the Co-op is a real resistance pattern, not a rebel headquarters.
    Table Scripts
    • If players ask whether Hush trusts them: "Not yet. But someone has put food near you, which means the door is not closed."
    • If corporate language appears at the table: "A local smiles politely and says, 'We do not sign care over to strangers here.'"
    Public Face
    A cheerful Hush village mutual: shared ale, shared tools, shared stories, and a four-leaf clover painted where neighbors gather.
    Real Function
    Keeps Hush out of corporate grip by pooling labor, barter, memory, and favors instead of signing every contract offered.
    Place In Story
    Act 3 sanctuary, local lore conduit, and the warm proof that not everyone has been bought.
    Disposition
    Warm but watchful; sincerity opens doors and corporate posture closes them.
    The Lever
    Respect Hush's independence and speak honestly; the Co-op rewards kindness faster than authority.

    Read Aloud

    Long Place Description
    The Clover Co-op is alive like a kitchen during a storm: warm, crowded, useful, nosy, and full of small systems players can engage with. The place breaks into usable table spaces: Taproom: Mismatched tables, card games, and low voices make the main room social terrain. Counter shelves: Jars, tools, notes, and tabs show what the community trades. Back kitchen: Steam, knives, bread, and practical staff movement keep the place from freezing into exposition. Notice board: Handwritten local notices reveal village priorities. Plants and materials: drying herbs, moss-honey combs, edible mushroom strings. Animals and background life: a tiny hearth-lizard stealing heat, market beetles under crates, moss hens near the back door. The air carries soup steam, card taps, jar clinks, floorboard gossip squeaks. Morning supplies, afternoon gossip, evening food, and late-night worry all pass through this room. What people can get here: food, moss-honey, rumors, minor supplies, community introductions.
    Known For
    Shoes come off at the door, and by the time you leave someone has tucked a little gift into them, a coin, a sweet, a folded note. Mismatched tables hold card games and lowered voices. Jars and tabs and handwritten notices crowd the shelves. Steam rolls out of the back kitchen where bread and knives keep a steady rhythm, and the floor squeaks in the exact spots everyone has learned to step around.
    Culture / Food
    Soup is always going, thick with moss-greens and whatever the farms could spare. Moss-honey comes by the comb. Tea arrives without being ordered, in a cup that does not match its saucer. The bread is cut thick and handed over while it is still too hot to hold properly.
    Travel Texture
    You smell it before the door opens, soup and damp wool and polished leather. Inside the noise closes around you, cards and jars and gossip, and a chair gets nudged out with someone's foot before anyone bothers to say hello.

    #rules-canon

    Action Roll Flow

    ['roll', 'check', 'skill check', 'action rolls'] ['saving throw', 'save', 'reaction'] ['group action', 'group action roll', 'group roll', 'team action']

    Ask what the PC does, choose the trait that matches the fictional approach, set or reveal the Difficulty, then roll.

    Roll Duality Dice, add the trait and any applicable Experience or bonus, compare the total to the Difficulty, then read Hope/Fear spin.

    Roll only when the outcome is uncertain and the consequence matters. If there is no meaningful pressure, give the result without a roll.

    #rules-canon

    Duality Dice

    ['2d12', 'hope die', 'fear die']

    Official default: roll two d12s, one Hope die and one Fear die.

    Total the dice, add the relevant trait and bonuses, then compare to Difficulty.

    The higher die determines whether the roll is with Hope or with Fear. Matching dice are a Critical Success.

    Reaction rolls use the Duality Dice structure, but they do not generate Hope or Fear.

    #rules-canon

    Hope/Fear Outcomes

    TotalSpinResult
    >= DifficultyHopeSuccess with Hope: get what you want and gain Hope.
    >= DifficultyFearSuccess with Fear: get what you want, but there is a cost or consequence; GM gains Fear.
    < DifficultyHopeFailure with Hope: things do not go as planned, but gain Hope.
    < DifficultyFearFailure with Fear: things go badly; major consequence or complication; GM gains Fear.

    #rules-canon

    Critical Success

    ['crit', 'critical', 'doubles']

    When the Hope die and Fear die match, the roll is a Critical Success.

    A Critical Success counts as a roll with Hope even if the total would otherwise miss the Difficulty.

    On a Critical Success, the PC gains Hope and clears 1 Stress. On an attack roll, follow the official critical damage rules.

    D&D-to-Daggerheart Translation

    D&D Conversion

    Daggerheart uses traits, not D&D skills. Pick the trait that matches the player's fictional approach.

    Use the D&D conversion page as a GM aid for players arriving from 5e; it is not official Daggerheart rules text.

    #rules-canon

    Advantage / Disadvantage

    ['advantage', 'disadvantage']

    Advantage adds a d6 advantage die to the roll total.

    Disadvantage subtracts a d6 disadvantage die from the roll total.

    Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out before rolling. If multiple advantage dice remain, use the highest one.

    The GM may grant advantage or disadvantage when the fiction supports it.

    #rules-canon

    Experiences, Help, Tag Team

    Experiences are short backstory/expertise phrases. Spend 1 Hope before a roll to add a relevant Experience modifier.

    Help an Ally: spend 1 Hope, describe feasible help, roll a d6 advantage die, and add it to the acting PC's roll using the normal advantage rules.

    Tag Team Roll: once per session, spend 3 Hope to combine two PCs' efforts. Both roll, choose one result for both, and resolve as a single action roll.

    #rules-canon

    Hope Uses / Fear Uses

    ['with Hope', 'hope spend', 'spend Hope'] ['Sablewood Hope Moments', 'Sablewood Fear Moments', 'hope moments', 'fear moments']

    Hope can be spent to Help an Ally, use Experiences, initiate a Tag Team Roll, or activate Hope Features.

    On a roll with Fear, the GM gains Fear and makes a move reflecting the complication.

    Fear can be spent to spotlight adversaries, use Fear Features, make stronger GM moves, apply adversary Experiences, and advance pressure when the rules or stat block call for it.

    #rules-canon

    GM Moves / Adversary Spotlight

    ['GM Move', 'make a move'] ['rules drawer', 'quick reference'] GM MovesAdversary Spotlight

    The spotlight moves through the fiction rather than a fixed initiative order.

    The GM commonly makes moves when a PC rolls with Fear or fails an action roll.

    Use softer moves to telegraph, reveal, or reposition. Use harder moves when the fiction and result call for direct consequence.

    Spend Fear or use features when the adversary/environment rules call for it.

    #rules-canon

    Adversary Attack Rolls

    ['enemy attack', 'attack roll', 'adversary attack'] Adversary Attack Rolls

    Adversaries roll a d20 plus their attack modifier against the target PC's Evasion.

    PCs do not roll defense by default. Use reaction rolls only when a rule, feature, or GM call gives one.

    PC action rolls against adversaries use the adversary's Difficulty from the stat block.

    #rules-canon

    Damage / Armor / Stress

    ['damage', 'threshold', 'thresholds'] ['falling', 'fall damage', 'collision', 'crash', 'dangerous speed'] ['damage die', 'damage dice', 'weapon damage'] Damage / Armor

    Compare incoming damage to the character's damage thresholds to determine how many HP are marked.

    Mark an Armor Slot to reduce the severity of incoming damage by one threshold.

    When a character marks their last Stress, they become Vulnerable until they clear at least 1 Stress.

    If a character must mark Stress while all Stress slots are marked, follow the official overflow consequence from the character rules.

    #rules-canon

    Short Rest

    ['rest', 'Prepare', 'Tend to Wounds', 'Clear Stress', 'Repair Armor']

    A short rest is about an hour to catch breath and recover.

    Each PC may swap domain cards between loadout and vault, then choose two moves, including choosing the same move twice.

    Short rest moves: Tend to Wounds, Clear Stress, Repair Armor, or Prepare.

    Prepare gains Hope; preparing with one or more party members gives each of those PCs 2 Hope.

    #rules-canon + module tracker

    Countdown

    ['clock', 'timer', 'countdown']

    Countdowns are visible pressure clocks. Tell players what changes when a countdown ticks.

    On progress countdowns, successful action rolls can tick the party closer to the goal.

    On consequence countdowns, failures or success with Fear can tick the opposing pressure closer.

    Open Vale uses a module ritual countdown as a tracker; do not treat it as a new condition.

    #rules-canon

    Ranges

    ['range', 'movement', 'distance']

    Use Daggerheart range bands at the table: Melee, Very Close, Close, Far, Very Far, and Out of Range.

    When exact distance is not important, keep ranges conversational and tied to reachable action.

    #rules-canon

    Official Conditions

    Official Conditions

    Daggerheart has three primary global conditions: Hidden, Restrained, and Vulnerable.

    Some features create unique effects, but those are feature-specific text, not module-wide conditions.

    Unless a rule says otherwise, the same condition cannot stack on one target.

    Temporary conditions can be cleared with a fitting action roll, a GM-approved fictional method, or by an adversary spending its spotlight to clear the condition.

    Unconscious appears only as Death Move/status context in this Atlas, not as a global condition card.
