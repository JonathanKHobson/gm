Hush

A real village under corporate designation, protected by ward pillars.

Aliases: Hush, Hush village, local community, the community, the local village, the village, forest village, small village

Source Reference

One-shot source PDF Source pages: Sablewood PDF p. 4

Player-Facing Description

A warm warded community of food, repairs, arguments, Firstmoss glow, shoes-off customs, and people trying to keep ordinary life ordinary.

Imagine This Like

A close-knit canal village like a tiny Venice or a bayou town where you are family by the third night. Think small fishing community, shoes-off-at-the-door, and the neighbor who already knows your business.

GM-Only Truth

Hush is never the joke. The joke is the system that renamed a real place as a managed endpoint and underfunded its protection.

Role in Story

Make Hush feel worth protecting before the finale asks the party to protect it.

Appearance / Sensory Palette

Bread, wet clover, repaired chairs, festival lanterns, children watching from behind trusted adults, ward chimes slightly off-key.

Wants / Pressures

Another season of safety, crops, music, ordinary life, and visitors who use the old name before the corporate one.

Fears / Vulnerabilities

Ward failure, outsiders treating hospitality as a service, and children knowing the brand before the place.

Read-Aloud

After the road and forest pressure, the destination finally becomes a village: warm windows, low roofs, clover smoke, repaired thresholds, and ward posts humming around ordinary people trying to keep ordinary life intact. This is Hush.

Sensory Palette

  • Sight: Low roofs, shoe lines, kitchen windows, repair benches, clover charms, and ward posts make the village feel guarded but lived in.
  • Sound: Mugs clink indoors, children whisper behind trusted adults, a distant work song falters when the ward hum misses a beat.
  • Smell: Bread, wet clover, hearth smoke, clean laundry, and a mineral edge from ward fixtures working too hard.
  • Texture / air: Packed earth is softened by moss; doorframes are polished by hands; the air warms near kitchens and cools near ward posts.
  • Background life: People notice everything without making a scene. Dogs, children, elders, and workers keep adjusting around the party's choices.
GM Location / Battlefield Notes
GM Orientation

The roleplay and breather hub. A warded village of warmth and music where the party must find the Whitefire Custodian by talking to locals. This is the social heart of the one-shot — three named NPCs, a vertical tavern, an optional festival, and the strongest "ask the players to build it with you" beat in the module.

    Imagine This Like
    A close-knit canal village like a tiny Venice or a bayou town where you are family by the third night. Think small fishing community, shoes-off-at-the-door, and the neighbor who already knows your business.
    Read Aloud
    Hush is warm before it is safe: low roofs, clover smoke, hand-repaired doors, and ward posts humming just a little off-key.
    Long Place Description
    Hush feels walkable in every direction: canals, porches, co-op warmth, moss farms, quiet gossip, low bridges, laundry, water gates, and neighbors who know the sound of every footstep. The place breaks into usable table spaces: Canal arrival: Low bridges and lantern reflections make the first approach soft and watchful. Porch ring: Homes face shared paths more than private yards. Clover Co-op corner: The tavern/co-op is the social stomach of the village. Moss-water lanes: Narrow plank paths lead toward farms and irrigation. Quiet old shrine: A small mossy place holds older local memory. Plants and materials: firstmoss, glowmoss, canal reeds, soft roof moss, kitchen herbs in hanging jars. Animals and background life: Ratcoons work the canals and porch edges, Festival Moths drift near moss lanterns, Young Dryads watch hospitality from the tree line, small fish flash under planks. The air carries low voices carry over water, lantern reflections double every movement, wet wood smell, soft ward hum near water gates. Hush eats, farms, repairs, gossips, and watches; the village is quiet because everyone is listening. What people can get here: Clover Co-op food and drink, moss-honey jars, minor repairs, local directions, quiet gossip.
    Known For
    Moss softens every lane until your footsteps go quiet, so you hear the canals first, then laundry snapping on a line, then a hundred small repairs underway at once. Doors hang a little crooked and patched with obvious care. On the ward posts at the water gates a low hum runs just off-key, and nobody flinches at it anymore. Somewhere bread is always in an oven. People glance up and seem to know whose boots are whose.
    Culture / Food
    The kettle is already on when you arrive, because it always is. Moss-honey ends up in everything, the tea, the bread, the bottom of a thank-you. Canal fish come up small and silver and get fried whole. A neighbor hands a bowl across a low bridge without breaking the sentence they were in the middle of.
    Travel Texture
    The road gives way to packed earth and then to moss, and your steps stop making sound. Voices carry over the water instead, low and in no hurry. Lantern light doubles on the black canal so every movement happens twice. You feel watched for a moment, then realize it is only that everyone here notices everything.

    #rules-canon

    Action Roll Flow

    ['roll', 'check', 'skill check', 'action rolls'] ['saving throw', 'save', 'reaction']

    Ask what the PC does, choose the trait that matches the fictional approach, set or reveal the Difficulty, then roll.

    Roll Duality Dice, add the trait and any applicable Experience or bonus, compare the total to the Difficulty, then read Hope/Fear spin.

    Roll only when the outcome is uncertain and the consequence matters. If there is no meaningful pressure, give the result without a roll.

    #rules-canon

    Duality Dice

    ['2d12', 'hope die', 'fear die']

    Official default: roll two d12s, one Hope die and one Fear die.

    Total the dice, add the relevant trait and bonuses, then compare to Difficulty.

    The higher die determines whether the roll is with Hope or with Fear. Matching dice are a Critical Success.

    Reaction rolls use the Duality Dice structure, but they do not generate Hope or Fear.

    #rules-canon

    Hope/Fear Outcomes

    TotalSpinResult
    >= DifficultyHopeSuccess with Hope: get what you want and gain Hope.
    >= DifficultyFearSuccess with Fear: get what you want, but there is a cost or consequence; GM gains Fear.
    < DifficultyHopeFailure with Hope: things do not go as planned, but gain Hope.
    < DifficultyFearFailure with Fear: things go badly; major consequence or complication; GM gains Fear.

    #rules-canon

    Critical Success

    ['crit', 'critical', 'doubles']

    When the Hope die and Fear die match, the roll is a Critical Success.

    A Critical Success counts as a roll with Hope even if the total would otherwise miss the Difficulty.

    On a Critical Success, the PC gains Hope and clears 1 Stress. On an attack roll, follow the official critical damage rules.

    D&D-to-Daggerheart Translation

    D&D Conversion

    Daggerheart uses traits, not D&D skills. Pick the trait that matches the player's fictional approach.

    Use the D&D conversion page as a GM aid for players arriving from 5e; it is not official Daggerheart rules text.

    #rules-canon

    Advantage / Disadvantage

    ['advantage', 'disadvantage']

    Advantage adds a d6 advantage die to the roll total.

    Disadvantage subtracts a d6 disadvantage die from the roll total.

    Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out before rolling. If multiple advantage dice remain, use the highest one.

    The GM may grant advantage or disadvantage when the fiction supports it.

    #rules-canon

    Experiences, Help, Tag Team

    Experiences are short backstory/expertise phrases. Spend 1 Hope before a roll to add a relevant Experience modifier.

    Help an Ally: spend 1 Hope, describe feasible help, roll a d6 advantage die, and add it to the acting PC's roll using the normal advantage rules.

    Tag Team Roll: once per session, spend 3 Hope to combine two PCs' efforts. Both roll, choose one result for both, and resolve as a single action roll.

    #rules-canon

    Hope Uses / Fear Uses

    ['with Hope', 'hope spend', 'spend Hope'] ['Sablewood Hope Moments', 'Sablewood Fear Moments', 'hope moments', 'fear moments']

    Hope can be spent to Help an Ally, use Experiences, initiate a Tag Team Roll, or activate Hope Features.

    On a roll with Fear, the GM gains Fear and makes a move reflecting the complication.

    Fear can be spent to spotlight adversaries, use Fear Features, make stronger GM moves, apply adversary Experiences, and advance pressure when the rules or stat block call for it.

    #rules-canon

    GM Moves / Adversary Spotlight

    ['GM Move', 'make a move'] ['rules drawer', 'quick reference'] GM MovesAdversary Spotlight

    The spotlight moves through the fiction rather than a fixed initiative order.

    The GM commonly makes moves when a PC rolls with Fear or fails an action roll.

    Use softer moves to telegraph, reveal, or reposition. Use harder moves when the fiction and result call for direct consequence.

    Spend Fear or use features when the adversary/environment rules call for it.

    #rules-canon

    Adversary Attack Rolls

    ['enemy attack', 'attack roll', 'adversary attack'] Adversary Attack Rolls

    Adversaries roll a d20 plus their attack modifier against the target PC's Evasion.

    PCs do not roll defense by default. Use reaction rolls only when a rule, feature, or GM call gives one.

    PC action rolls against adversaries use the adversary's Difficulty from the stat block.

    #rules-canon

    Damage / Armor / Stress

    ['damage', 'threshold', 'thresholds'] ['damage die', 'damage dice', 'weapon damage'] Damage / Armor

    Compare incoming damage to the character's damage thresholds to determine how many HP are marked.

    Mark an Armor Slot to reduce the severity of incoming damage by one threshold.

    When a character marks their last Stress, they become Vulnerable until they clear at least 1 Stress.

    If a character must mark Stress while all Stress slots are marked, follow the official overflow consequence from the character rules.

    #rules-canon

    Short Rest

    ['rest', 'Prepare', 'Tend to Wounds', 'Clear Stress', 'Repair Armor']

    A short rest is about an hour to catch breath and recover.

    Each PC may swap domain cards between loadout and vault, then choose two moves, including choosing the same move twice.

    Short rest moves: Tend to Wounds, Clear Stress, Repair Armor, or Prepare.

    Prepare gains Hope; preparing with one or more party members gives each of those PCs 2 Hope.

    #rules-canon + module tracker

    Countdown

    ['clock', 'timer', 'countdown']

    Countdowns are visible pressure clocks. Tell players what changes when a countdown ticks.

    On progress countdowns, successful action rolls can tick the party closer to the goal.

    On consequence countdowns, failures or success with Fear can tick the opposing pressure closer.

    Open Vale uses a module ritual countdown as a tracker; do not treat it as a new condition.

    #rules-canon

    Ranges

    ['range', 'movement', 'distance']

    Use Daggerheart range bands at the table: Melee, Very Close, Close, Far, Very Far, and Out of Range.

    When exact distance is not important, keep ranges conversational and tied to reachable action.

    #rules-canon

    Official Conditions

    Official Conditions

    Daggerheart has three primary global conditions: Hidden, Restrained, and Vulnerable.

    Some features create unique effects, but those are feature-specific text, not module-wide conditions.

    Unless a rule says otherwise, the same condition cannot stack on one target.

    Temporary conditions can be cleared with a fitting action roll, a GM-approved fictional method, or by an adversary spending its spotlight to clear the condition.

    Unconscious appears only as Death Move/status context in this Atlas, not as a global condition card.
